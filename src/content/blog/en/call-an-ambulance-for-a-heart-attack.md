**Call an ambulance at once if someone has chest pain or pressure that lasts more than a few minutes, or comes with sweating, breathlessness or pain spreading to the arm or jaw.** Do not wait to see if it passes.

This page is general information, not medical advice. In an emergency do this: call, share your location and the patient's condition. Then follow the dispatcher and the crew. In a life-threatening emergency in Bangladesh you can also call 999.

## Warning signs

- **Chest pain, pressure or tightness** that does not go away
- **Pain that spreads** to the arm, shoulder, neck, jaw or back
- **Shortness of breath**
- **Cold sweat, nausea or light-headedness**
- **Unusual tiredness or discomfort** without clear cause. These can be the only signs in some people, including women and people with diabetes.

If you are unsure, call. A dispatcher would rather take a call that turns out not to be serious.

## Why call instead of driving

- The crew starts checking the heart the moment they arrive.
- They can tell the hospital you are coming, so the team is ready.
- If the patient's condition changes on the road, trained people are with them.

## What to tell the dispatcher

1. **Where you are.** Area, road and a landmark. Say if the building has a lift or the patient is upstairs.
2. **Who is unwell.** Age and what they feel.
3. **Known conditions.** Heart disease, diabetes, blood pressure.
4. **A phone number** that stays on.
5. **The hospital**, if a doctor has already named one.

## While you wait

- Stay with the patient.
- Open the door and gate so the crew can come straight in.
- Keep their medicines and any hospital papers together.
- Keep the phone line free.

## What happens next

The crew checks the patient and may record an ECG, then moves them to hospital.
