**An ICU ambulance lets a patient keep intensive-care level support while moving from one hospital to another.** Without it, a very unwell patient could go without monitoring and support for the length of the trip.

## What it is

A vehicle set up with a monitor, oxygen and, depending on the vehicle, a ventilator and drip pumps, with a crew trained to run them. It is not a hospital room, and it cannot treat everything. It keeps the patient supported and watched until the destination hospital takes over. See [ICU ambulance equipment](/essential-equipment-is-carried-by-ambulance-crews/).

## When a doctor advises one

- The patient is on a ventilator or oxygen that cannot be interrupted
- The patient needs close monitoring of heart or blood pressure
- The patient needs a drip or medicine pump to keep running
- The receiving hospital has an ICU bed and the current one does not

Only the treating doctor can say whether a patient needs an ICU ambulance. Ask the doctor, and tell us in the doctor's words.

## Why it matters in Dhaka

Traffic can be slow and unpredictable, and a transfer can take longer than planned. A patient who loses support in a stalled vehicle is at risk. A prepared vehicle and crew reduce that risk. They do not remove it. The dispatcher tells you honestly when a delay is likely.

## What to ask before you book

1. What is on board: monitor, ventilator, pumps?
2. Who travels with the patient and what is their training? See [crew training](/what-is-required-for-ambulance-crew-members/).
3. Is the destination hospital expecting the patient?
4. What is the full fare, with waiting, and is it told before the vehicle leaves?

## Gaps to watch

Not every vehicle called "ICU" is equipped alike. Equipment, crew and fares vary. Ask for specifics and compare. For what an ICU is, read [what an ICU does](/understanding-the-role-of-intensive-care-units/).
