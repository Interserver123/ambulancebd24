**Choose by what the patient needs and how soon, not by who runs the vehicle.** Government and private ambulances each suit some cases.

## The short comparison

| | Government or hospital-run | Private service |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Usually lower | Usually higher, depends on vehicle and distance |
| Booking | Through the hospital or its own line | Direct phone call, often 24 hours |
| Choice of vehicle | Limited to what is available | AC, ICU, NICU or freezer van by need |
| Long routes | Often limited to the hospital's area | Many take district trips |
| Equipment and crew | Varies | Varies |

These are general patterns, not rules. Both kinds differ a lot from one operator to another.

## When a government or hospital ambulance fits

- The patient is already in that hospital and it offers a transfer
- A short trip, and the fare matters most
- The hospital can say when a vehicle will be free

## When a private ambulance fits

- You need a specific vehicle, for example an [ICU ambulance](/icu-ambulance-service-dhaka/) or [NICU ambulance](/nicu-ambulance-service-dhaka/)
- You need it now or at night
- The trip is long, for example to another district: see [Dhaka to any district](/inter-district-ambulance-service/)

## Questions to ask either way

1. What vehicle is coming, and who travels with the patient?
2. When will it arrive?
3. What is the full fare, with waiting, and is it told before it leaves?
4. Can a family member ride along?

If the provider will not answer plainly, call another.
