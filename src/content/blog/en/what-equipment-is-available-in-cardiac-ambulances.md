**A cardiac ambulance does not treat a heart attack on its own. It watches the heart, steadies the patient and moves them quickly to a hospital that can treat it.** The equipment exists for those three jobs.

This is a general guide. It describes what heart-patient transport usually needs, not the vehicles of any one provider. For a transfer with us, tell the dispatcher what the doctor advised and we match the [ICU ambulance](/icu-ambulance-service-dhaka/).

## Equipment that watches the heart

- **ECG (electrocardiogram).** Records the heart's electrical signal. A 12-lead ECG gives the clearest picture and can be sent ahead to the hospital.
- **Continuous monitor.** Heart rate, blood pressure and blood oxygen on one screen, checked throughout the trip.

## Equipment that restores a rhythm

- **Defibrillator.** Gives a shock if the heart goes into a dangerous rhythm. Automated models (AED) guide the user step by step. Manual models are used by trained advanced crews.

## Equipment that supports breathing

- **Oxygen** with masks and tubing, as the doctor advises.
- **Suction and airway tools**, kept within reach of the head end.
- **A bag-valve-mask** to breathe for a patient who cannot.

## Drips and medicines

Some crews can give medicines through a drip, for example for pain or heart rhythm, within their training and the doctor's orders. Whether a given ambulance has this depends on the provider and the crew, so ask.

## The vehicle itself

- A **stretcher that can be raised** to a sitting angle for patients who cannot lie flat
- **Secure fixing** for the monitor and oxygen cylinders
- **Good lighting and space** to work around the patient

## What an ambulance cannot do

It cannot replace the hospital. Treatments that open a blocked artery need a hospital with a cardiac unit. The ambulance's job is to get the patient there stable and with the hospital warned. See [what happens when an ambulance checks for a heart attack](/can-ambulance-check-for-heart-attack/).

## Questions to ask a provider

1. Is there a monitor with ECG, and a defibrillator?
2. Who travels with the patient, and what is their training? ([crew training guide](/what-is-required-for-ambulance-crew-members/))
3. Can the ambulance reach the hospital the doctor chose?
4. What is the full fare, told before the vehicle leaves?
