**Rules for ambulance crews differ by country and by employer, so the useful question is not "what is required" but "what has this crew completed".** Ask the provider to answer that before you agree to a trip.

This is a general guide to how crew training is usually built. It does not describe any one provider's crew. When you book our [ICU ambulance](/icu-ambulance-service-dhaka/) or any other service, the dispatcher tells you who travels with the patient before you agree.

## The usual levels of training

- **First aid and CPR.** The base. Chest compressions, rescue breaths, bleeding control. Often renewed every one to two years.
- **Basic life support (BLS).** Patient assessment, oxygen, an automated defibrillator (AED), splinting, safe moving and handover to the hospital.
- **Advanced life support (ALS).** Paramedic level. Drips and medicines, airway support, heart rhythm reading and treatment. It takes the longest and builds on BLS.
- **Trauma courses.** Road accidents and falls: spinal care, severe bleeding, shock.
- **Newborn and child transport.** Babies are not small adults. Their equipment, doses and warmth needs differ. See the [NICU ambulance guide](/nicu-equipped-ambulances-in-dhaka/).
- **Driving.** Emergency driving is its own skill: patient comfort, junctions, traffic.

## What matters more than the certificate

A certificate shows a course was passed. It does not show how someone acts at 3 a.m. with a frightened family. Look for:

1. **Recent training.** Skills fade. Ask when the crew last renewed.
2. **Hands-on hours** with real patients, under supervision.
3. **Calm communication.** The crew should tell you what they are doing and why.
4. **A doctor on call** for transfers that need one.

## Questions to ask before you book

- Who travels with the patient: a driver only, or a trained attendant?
- What training has that person completed, and when?
- Does the crew match the patient's need, for example a newborn or a heart patient?
- Can a family member ride along?

If the answer is vague, ask again or call another provider.
