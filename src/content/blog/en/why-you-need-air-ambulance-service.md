**An air ambulance makes sense when the road trip is too long, too slow or too rough for the patient, and a doctor agrees the patient can fly.** It costs far more than a road ambulance, so compare before you decide.

We do not own aircraft. We [arrange air ambulance transfers](/air-ambulance-service-bangladesh/) through an aviation operator and take the patient to and from the airport in our ground ambulance.

## Eight situations where families consider it

1. **A very long distance**, for example from a far northern or south-eastern district to Dhaka.
2. **Roads that are slow or cut off** by flood, damage or heavy traffic.
3. **A patient who cannot bear a long road trip**, in the doctor's judgement.
4. **Treatment needed urgently** at a hospital that is hours away by road.
5. **A move abroad for treatment**, with a doctor-led handover at the other end.
6. **Return from abroad** to a hospital at home.
7. **A patient who needs continuous monitoring** that is easier on a short flight.
8. **A transfer between cities** where both hospitals are near an airport.

## Questions to settle first

- **Does the treating doctor agree the patient can fly?** Only a doctor can say.
- **Is there a bed waiting at the destination?** Ask the receiving hospital to confirm.
- **Who is the operator, and what is the full fare?** Ask the operator before you agree.
- **What do the two airport legs cost?** Ground transfers at each end add to the total.
- **What are the weather and daylight limits?** Flights can be delayed.

## Air or road?

For most trips between Dhaka and a district, a road ambulance is simpler and cheaper. See [Dhaka to any district](/inter-district-ambulance-service/). Choose air when time or road conditions make the road trip unsafe, and a doctor supports the move.
