**In an emergency, call, share your location and say the patient's condition.** Everything else can wait. In a life-threatening emergency you can also call 999.

## When to call right away

Call at once for chest pain, trouble breathing, heavy bleeding, a seizure, sudden weakness or loss of speech, a serious injury or someone who will not wake. If you are unsure, call. See [when to call for a heart attack](/call-an-ambulance-for-a-heart-attack/).

## What to say

1. **Where.** Area, road, building and a landmark. Send a WhatsApp location pin if you can.
2. **Who.** Age and what happened or what they feel.
3. **How serious.** Awake or not, breathing or not, bleeding or not.
4. **A phone number** that stays on.
5. **The hospital**, if a doctor has named one.

## Questions to ask the dispatcher

- How long until the ambulance arrives?
- Who travels with the patient?
- What is the full fare, and will you tell me before the ambulance leaves?

Our approved answer to the last one: we tell you the fare before we dispatch.

## While you wait

- Stay with the patient.
- Open the gate and the door, and switch on the lights on the stairs.
- Gather medicines, papers and the patient's ID.
- Ask one person to meet the ambulance at the road.

## Planned trips

Discharge, check-ups and district trips do not need the same rush. Call earlier, choose the right vehicle and agree the fare. See [types of ambulance in Dhaka](/list-of-ambulance-service-in-dhaka/) and [tips for finding a reliable service](/tips-to-find-ambulance-service/).

## Save the number now

Put **01919-339689** in your phone as "Ambulance". A dispatcher answers 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. See our [ICU ambulance service](/icu-ambulance-service-dhaka/) for hospital-to-hospital transfers.
